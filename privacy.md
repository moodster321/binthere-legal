# Privacy Policy

**Effective date:** [EFFECTIVE DATE: the date this update is published]

This Privacy Policy explains how Bin There Games LLC ("Bin There Games", "we", "us") handles
information in connection with our game **SEVER // ctrl** (the "Game"), including every version
of it (Steam, the Epic Games Store, the DRM-free download and the free demo), its online, Steam,
Epic and Discord features, its bug reports and anonymous stats, and this website
(legal.binthere.games).

## The short version

- **We don't run game servers or player accounts, and we don't advertise.**
- **Anonymous stats are off unless you say yes.** When you first start the Game, it asks whether
  it may send us anonymous stats about how you play: the units you build, the story choices you
  make and how each level ends. They carry a random install ID, never your name or account. You
  can change your mind at any time in Settings > Privacy.
- **Bug reports are sent only when you press Send.** Nothing is reported automatically. A report
  includes what you type and information about your computer and, unless you untick them, your
  logs and saved game, with your names and account IDs removed.
- **Online play uses Epic Online Services,** run by Epic Games, in every version of the Game. The
  Game signs in to it only when you first use online play. Epic then receives your Steam or Epic
  account ID (or, in the DRM-free version, an anonymous device ID and your display name), and
  the details of any lobby you host.
- **Discord** can show what you're doing in the Game. If you choose to connect your Discord
  account, the Game also reads your Discord name and friends list so you can invite friends.
- **Steam, Epic and Discord** handle what they receive under their own privacy policies.
- Some information is **stored only on your own computer** (settings, saves, log files).
- **We do not sell your personal information** or use it for advertising.
- Questions or requests: **[hello@binthere.games](mailto:hello@binthere.games)**.

## What information is involved, and where it goes

### Anonymous stats

If you agree, the Game sends us anonymous statistics about how people play. They help us balance
the Game. We receive and store them using PostHog, an analytics service, which handles them for
us as our service provider.

- **Off unless you say yes.** The first time you start the Game, it asks whether it may send
  stats. It sends nothing unless you answer yes. You can turn stats on or off at any time in
  Settings > Privacy.
- **What is sent:**
  - when you start playing (and whether it's your first session, and your graphics quality
    setting);
  - the units you build in the Unit Builder: their chassis, parts, abilities and traits, plus a
    short code for the design, without any names you typed;
  - the choices you make in the Game's own story dialogue;
  - which places on the campaign map you visit (levels, shops and the Unit Builder), and what
    you buy in shops;
  - when a level starts and ends: which level, the game mode, the units you took, whether you
    won, lost or left, how many rounds it took and how long. Levels that players made are
    recorded only as "custom" or "Workshop", never by their names.
- **Sent with every event:** the Game's version and build (full or demo, and which store version
  it is), your operating system, your language setting, whether you're on a handheld such as the
  Steam Deck, whether it is a development build, a random ID for that session and the order of
  events within it.
- **The random install ID.** The Game ties your events together with a random ID, created when
  you say yes. It is not linked to your name, your store account or your computer. If you later
  turn stats off, the Game deletes the ID and any stats not yet sent. If you turn them on again,
  it creates a new ID, so the two periods can't be linked.
- **Your IP address.** Like any internet connection, sending stats shows PostHog your IP address.
  We have set PostHog to discard it rather than store it, and the Game asks PostHog not to work
  out your location from it.
- **Not sent:** your name, account IDs, anything you type, and your saves or logs.
- **If stats can't be sent right away** (for example, when you're offline or you quit), the Game
  keeps them in a file on your computer and sends them the next time it starts.

PostHog stores the stats in the United States.

### Bug reports and feedback

You can send us a bug report or feedback from inside the Game, for example with Send Feedback on
the Game's desktop or from the pause menu. We receive reports through Sentry, an error-tracking
service, which handles them for us as our service provider.

- **Only when you press Send.** The Game never sends reports by itself, even when it crashes.
  After a crash, it offers to open a report for you, and nothing is sent unless you press Send.
- **Always included:**
  - the title, description and type (bug or feedback) you enter;
  - the Game's version and build;
  - information about your computer: operating system and version, device model, processor,
    memory, screen and window size, graphics card, renderer, graphics quality setting, language,
    and whether you're on a Steam Deck.
- **Included unless you untick them:** the Game's two most recent log files (this session's and
  the one before), and your saved game.
- **Only if you add them:** a screenshot of the Game that you take from the form, and images you
  attach. The Game re-encodes every image before sending it, which removes hidden information
  such as camera details and location (EXIF).
- **What the Game removes first.** Before sending logs and your save, the Game replaces:
  - the path of your home folder and your computer's account name;
  - your store display name and account ID (Steam, Epic, or the Display name in the DRM-free
    version), any SteamID, and your Epic Online Services ID;
  - the coded account ID in your saved game (see [On your computer](#on-your-computer)).

  Logs can still contain other details, such as lobby codes, other players' Epic Online Services
  IDs, IP addresses you typed in to join a game and the names of Remote Play guests. If you'd
  rather not send them, untick the logs.
- **No contact details.** A report has no email or user field. The Game shows you a random report
  number after sending, which you can quote if you email us. If you want a reply, email us.
- **Your IP address.** Like any internet connection, sending a report shows Sentry your IP
  address. We have set Sentry not to store it.

Sentry stores reports in the United States.

### Online play (Epic Online Services)

Online play in every version of the Game uses Epic Online Services, software and online services
provided by Epic Games, Inc. ("Epic"). It runs the online lobbies, finds games and connects
players, including players who got the Game from different stores ("crossplay"). Epic provides
these services for us as our service provider. The demo has no online lobbies and does not
include Epic Online Services.

- **When the Game signs in.** The Game signs you in to Epic Online Services only when you first
  use online play in a session, for example when you open the online lobbies or accept an
  invite. If you don't play online, the Game doesn't sign you in.
- **How you're identified.** To sign you in, the Game gives Epic:
  - in the **Steam version**, a Steam sign-in ticket, which Epic checks with Steam, so Epic learns
    your SteamID;
  - in the **Epic Games Store version**, a sign-in token from your Epic account (see
    [Epic Games Store version](#epic-games-store-version));
  - in the **DRM-free version**, or when your store isn't available, an anonymous device ID that
    Epic's software creates and keeps on your computer, together with your operating system and
    device model name and your display name.

  Epic then gives you an ID for the Game's online services (a "product user ID"). Other players'
  copies of the Game see that ID, not your store account ID.
- **Your display name** is your Steam or Epic display name. In the DRM-free version, it is the
  Display name you set in Settings > Online, or, if you leave that blank, a generated name such
  as "Operator-1A2B".
- **Lobbies.** When you host an online game, the Game creates a lobby that players of the same
  version of the Game, anywhere in the world, can find. The lobby shows:
  - its name (if you leave the name blank, the Game uses your display name);
  - the selected level;
  - the number of teams;
  - which store your copy of the Game comes from, and your crossplay setting;
  - a code for the Game's version, so that only matching versions find each other;
  - your product user ID, as the host.

  Players in a lobby can see one another's product user IDs. A lobby disappears when its host
  leaves.
- **Crossplay** is on by default. If you turn it off in Settings > Online, you only find and host
  games with players who got the Game from the same store.
- **Connections.** In a match, players' computers connect to each other ("peer-to-peer"); we
  don't run a server in between. When it can, Epic's software connects players directly, and
  then the players in the match can see one another's IP addresses. When a direct connection
  isn't possible, Epic relays the connection through its servers, and Epic sees the IP addresses
  instead. Epic also sees your IP address whenever the Game uses its services.
- **Invites.** Invites and "Join" buttons on Steam, Epic and Discord carry a code for the lobby.

Epic may process this information in the United States and other countries, and relies on the
European Commission's standard contractual clauses for transfers out of Europe. See the
[Epic Games Privacy Policy](https://www.epicgames.com/site/privacypolicy). To have the
information Epic holds for the Game's online play deleted, email us and we will use the tools
Epic gives developers for data requests.

### Epic Games Store version

If you play the Epic Games Store version, the Game also connects to your Epic Games account
through Epic Account Services.

- **Signing in.** When you start the Game from the Epic Games Launcher, the launcher signs the
  Game in to your Epic account. The first time, Epic shows you a consent screen that lists what
  the Game asks for: your basic profile (your Epic display name and account ID), your friends
  list and your online presence.
- **What the Game reads:** your Epic account ID and Epic display name. It uses them to sign you
  in to online play, as the default name of lobbies you host, in the coded account ID in your
  saved games, and in log files on your computer.
- **Your presence.** The Game tells Epic what you are doing, such as "In the menu" or "In a match"
  with the level name and, while you are in a lobby, what your friends need to join you. Epic
  shows this to your Epic friends.
- **Your friends list.** The Game does not read or store your friends list. The Epic overlay (on
  Windows) uses it to show your friends and let you invite and join them.

Epic handles your Epic account as an independent company under the
[Epic Games Privacy Policy](https://www.epicgames.com/site/privacypolicy). You can remove the
Game's access to your account in your Epic account settings. Our contact for data protection
questions about this data is [hello@binthere.games](mailto:hello@binthere.games).

### Steam

The Steam version uses Valve's Steamworks services.

- **Your Steam identity.** The Game reads your SteamID (your account number) and your Steam
  display name from the Steam app on your computer. It uses them to sign you in to online play
  (see [Online play](#online-play-epic-online-services)), as the default name of lobbies you
  host, for Steam Remote Play Together and in the coded account ID in your saved games, and they
  appear in
  log files on your computer (see [On your computer](#on-your-computer)). The Game removes them
  from bug reports and does not otherwise send them to us.
- **What you're doing in the Game.** The Game tells Steam your status, such as "In the menu",
  "In a lobby" or "In a match" with the level name. While you are in a lobby, it also gives Steam
  a code for the lobby, so your friends can use "Join Game" and invites. Steam shows this to your
  Steam friends, depending on your Steam privacy settings.
- **Remote Play Together.** If you invite friends with Remote Play Together, Steam streams the
  Game to them, and the Game sees their Steam names.
- **Steam Workshop.** If you publish something you made, such as a custom level or unit, to the
  Steam Workshop, the Game uploads it to Steam with its title, description, preview image and
  some technical details, such as the content format and the Game version. New items are
  **private** when first published; you choose whether friends or the public can see them.
  Published items are also covered by Steam's
  [Workshop Legal Agreement](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/workshoplegalagreement). If
  you subscribe to Workshop items, Steam downloads them to your computer.

Valve handles this information as an independent company under the
[Steam Privacy Policy](https://store.steampowered.com/privacy_agreement/). Valve also makes some
information available to us as the Game's developer, such as sales and usage reports and
whether an account owns the Game. We use it only to run, support and improve the Game.

### Discord

**Your activity.** If the Discord app is running on your computer, the Game sends it a short
note about what you are doing: the Game's name plus text such as "Main menu", "Campaign" or the
name of the level you are playing. While you are in an online lobby, the note also gives the
number of players in it and a code for the lobby, so Discord can show a Join button and carry
invites. Discord calls this "Rich Presence". Discord may show it on your profile to other Discord
users, depending on your Discord settings. On its own, this reads nothing from your Discord
account.

**If you connect your Discord account.** Connecting is your choice: in Settings > Online, choose
Connect Discord and approve the request in Discord. Once connected:

- The Game reads:
  - your Discord user ID, username and display name;
  - your Discord friends list: each friend's user ID, username, display name and online status,
    and whether they are playing the Game.
- It uses them to show which account is connected and to list your friends in the online lobby
  so you can invite them. When you invite a friend, Discord sends them an invite with the
  lobby's code.
- Your activity goes through Discord's servers, so it shows even when the Discord app is closed.
- The Game does not read your messages, servers or avatar, and doesn't store your friends list.
- So that you stay connected between sessions, the Game keeps a Discord sign-in token on your
  computer, encrypted with a key tied to that computer.
- **Disconnect** (in Settings > Online) deletes the token from your computer and asks Discord to
  cancel it. You can also remove the Game under Authorized Apps in Discord's settings.

**We do not receive** what the Game sends to or reads from Discord. Discord handles it under the
[Discord Privacy Policy](https://discord.com/privacy).

### Playing directly by IP address

You can also host or join a game by IP address (LAN / Direct IP), without Epic Online Services.
The players in that match can see one another's IP addresses, because their computers connect
directly. Only connect directly with people you trust.

### During a match

During any multiplayer match, the players' copies of the Game exchange gameplay information:
moves, the state of the match, a check that everyone has the same version of the Game, and a
temporary random code that lets you rejoin if you get disconnected. None of this is kept after
the match. The Game has **no text or voice chat**.

### On your computer

The Game keeps these files in its own folder on your computer:

- **Windows:** `%APPDATA%\sever-ctrl\`
- **Linux:** `~/.local/share/sever-ctrl/`
- **macOS:** `~/Library/Application Support/sever-ctrl/`

The demo uses a folder named `sever-ctrl-demo` instead. The files are:

- `settings.cfg` and `keybindings.cfg`: your settings and key bindings, including your Display
  name and crossplay setting;
- `player_save.tres` and `quicksave.tres`: your campaign progress and saved games;
- `floppy_designs/` and `workshop/`: designs and levels you create in the Game, including text
  you type into them, plus Workshop items you are preparing or have downloaded;
- `analytics.cfg`: your answer about stats and, while stats are on, the random install ID;
- `analytics_pending.json`: stats waiting to be sent, if any;
- `discord_auth.cfg`: the encrypted Discord sign-in token, if you connected Discord;
- `eos-cache/`: files that Epic's software keeps for online play;
- `logs/`: log files of what the Game is doing in your last five sessions, which can include:
  - your SteamID and Steam display name, or your Epic display name;
  - your product user ID, other players' product user IDs and lobby codes;
  - IP addresses and ports you typed in to join a game;
  - the names of Remote Play guests;
- a few small technical files, such as which version of the Game last ran.

**Saved games also contain a coded copy of your store account ID:** your SteamID in the Steam
version, your Epic account ID in the Epic Games Store version, and no account ID in the DRM-free
version, along with which store version wrote the save. It is there to identify where a saved
game came from if a copy is leaked or shared without permission and reaches us. The Game never
sends it anywhere, and removes it from saved games attached to bug reports.

The Game uploads none of these files, except the logs and saved game you choose to attach to a
bug report and, if you agreed to stats, the stats waiting to be sent. If Steam Cloud saving is
turned on for the Game, Steam may keep a copy of your saved games in your Steam account, under
Valve's privacy policy.

You can delete these files at any time; doing so resets your settings and progress.

## How long information is kept

- **Anonymous stats** are kept for up to one year, then deleted automatically.
- **Bug reports** are kept for 30 days, then deleted automatically.
- **Online play:** a lobby disappears when its host leaves. Epic keeps your product user ID and
  the account or device ID it is linked to until they are deleted, either under Epic's own rules
  or when you ask us to delete them.
- **Emails** are kept as long as we need them to deal with your message, and no longer than two
  years after the conversation ends, unless the law requires us to keep them longer.
- **The Discord sign-in token** stays on your computer until you disconnect Discord or delete the
  file.
- **Files on your computer** stay until you delete them or uninstall the Game.
- **Information held by Steam, Discord or Epic for your Epic account** is kept under their own
  policies.

## Your choices and rights

- **Stats:** turn them on or off at any time in Settings > Privacy. Turning them off deletes the
  install ID and any stats not yet sent.
- **Bug reports:** nothing is sent unless you press Send, and you can untick the logs and saved
  game before you do.
- **Online play:** if you don't use online play, the Game doesn't sign you in to Epic Online
  Services. You can also play directly by IP address instead.
- **Crossplay:** turn it off in Settings > Online to play only with players from the same store.
- **Discord:** turn off Show My Activity on Discord in Settings > Online, or turn off activity
  sharing in Discord's Activity Privacy settings. If you connected your account, choose
  Disconnect in Settings > Online, or remove the Game under Authorized Apps in Discord.
- **Epic account:** remove the Game's access in your Epic account settings, or change who sees
  your presence in Epic's privacy settings.
- **Steam:** change your Steam privacy settings, or appear offline or invisible, to hide your
  status from friends.
- **Steam Workshop:** change who can see your items, or delete them, on Steam.
- **Files on your computer:** delete them at any time (see [On your computer](#on-your-computer)).

Depending on where you live, for example in the EEA, the UK or California, you may have the
right to:

- ask what personal information we have about you, and get a copy;
- have it corrected or deleted;
- object to or restrict how we use it;
- take back any consent you gave;
- not be treated differently for using these rights.

To make a request, email [hello@binthere.games](mailto:hello@binthere.games). We will reply
within one month, or sooner if the law requires. We hold little that identifies you: stats carry
only a random install ID, and bug reports have your names and account IDs removed. If your
request is about stats or a bug report, include your install ID (from `analytics.cfg`, while
stats are on) or your report number so we can find them. For information Epic holds for online
play, we use the tools Epic gives developers. Information held by Steam, Discord or Epic for your
Epic account is controlled through their own privacy tools, and we're glad to point you to the
right place. You can also complain to your local data protection authority (in the UK, the
Information Commissioner's Office).
